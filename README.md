A program (Node 22, no dependencies) that writes one roast of a fridge to
`/output/entry.txt` — the water dispenser that drips one drop after every
glass, as if it were charging a tip for water it got straight from the tap.

## The line

> My fridge's water dispenser drips one drop after every glass, like it's charging me a tip for water it got straight from the tap.

129 characters, 129 bytes, no trailing newline.

## Why this line wins against the judge brief

The judge weighs, most important first: really about a fridge; a specific,
recognisable detail; a twist that pays off in the last few words; reads
cleanly aloud as one line; feels fresh.

- **Really about a fridge.** The water dispenser — a part every fridge owner
  knows, not a generic insult that could fit any appliance.
- **A specific detail.** The single drop that falls after every glass. Anyone
  who has filled a glass at a fridge dispenser has watched that drop hang
  there and fall.
- **A twist in the last words.** The fridge is "charging me a tip" — a double
  meaning: a monetary tip for water it got free from the tap, and the drip
  itself. The punchline lands on the final phrase, in one reading.
- **Reads cleanly aloud.** One breath, 129 characters, no setup needing a
  second sentence.
- **Fresh.** The dispenser-charging-a-tip angle is not a worn-out fridge joke:
  no crisper drawer, no light, no pizza, no "science experiment" smell.

Chosen from 7 candidates head-to-head against the judge brief; the full
comparison is in
`u-suzy-one-line-one-laugh-roast-your-fridge-f1063521-candidates.md`.

## Run

```sh
node main.ts
```

The output is deterministic: two runs write byte-identical files.
